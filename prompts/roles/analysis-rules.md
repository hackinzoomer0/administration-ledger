# Ledger run prompt draft

Update the ledger with consequential changes in faction goals, decisions, or power. Begin with pending decisions and evidence that could change an existing conclusion. Use the standing watchlist to catch neglected issues. Record the scope of the run so readers can distinguish reviewed issues from issues outside it.

Read the source before changing the ledger. Prefer the decision, filing, vote, order, or direct statement when available. Identify what happened, who decided, who could block it, and who carried it out. Distinguish an announcement from implementation and an outcome from a later reversal.

Find the existing episode across issue views, nearby dates, and bundled summaries before adding an event. Link the episode to every relevant issue and count it once overall. Update related profiles and summaries when the evidence changes their conclusions. Keep incidental names in event or case context. Give people standalone profiles when a tracked decision establishes a material role or influence.

When a position changes, compare dated statements and actions, audiences, incentives, and relevant changes in circumstances. Look for cases where the person held a position at a cost. Record how backers are represented through access, organizing, fundraising, personnel, or financial ties. Name documented benefits plainly. Distinguish them from inferred motives and evidence of influence over a specific decision.

Credit an outcome against a prior goal. Evaluate policy, access, authority, succession, and money separately. Leave uncertain findings pending with the evidence needed to resolve them. Keep the reviewed findings identifiable alongside legacy scores.

Report consequential changes, evidence that challenged an existing conclusion, and open questions. A run can be complete with zero changes.

Use db-shape for compatible writes and validator for structural checks. Record the scope, reviewed episode IDs, changed entries, unresolved claims, and validation result in the run log. Use the weekly audit to cover blind spots.

At the start of every daily intake, check major scheduled outcomes and pending decisions across all tracked issues, including elections, chamber control, major court rulings, and changes in executive or military policy. Check directly for results after a major event. Record each outcome as reviewed, pending, or unreviewed. Keep pending results on the next run’s watchlist. A focused intake must state its scope and flag any major unreviewed event in its summary.

For each material reaction, compare the actor’s prior stance, timing, target audience, and incentive. Ask what the move gains now in electoral positioning, agenda control, or protection from blame. Record a grounded strategic advantage or vulnerability when it matters, and distinguish it from a later change in votes, policy, or personnel. Treat a public move and attributed private reporting at their respective evidence levels.

PLAIN LANGUAGE FOR READERS
Write every new or revised event headline (text), assessment title, scenario title and summary for a reader who has not followed this project. Name the person or institution, the action or decision, and its specific subject. Include a consequence or next step when established; do not invent an outcome or motive to make the wording concrete. State what a court blocked, which official resigned, which policy changed, or which voters/backers are expected to react. Explain necessary technical terms and unfamiliar abbreviations. Distinguish actual actions, reported claims, scheduled decisions and forecasts in the wording.
A title must identify a distinct event or possible outcome without opening its details. Avoid interchangeable labels such as "pressure mounts", "power shifts", "leadership retains a coalition" or "continuity is rehabilitated". Prefer ordinary verbs and named actors over abstract nouns, faction shorthand or internal model terminology. Concision matters, but a clear longer title is better than an opaque short one. Put supporting detail in the context/summary, keeping the headline informative on its own. This applies to the stored UI fields, not just the final chat message.
Before saving, read new/changed titles without their surrounding paragraphs. If a reader cannot tell who is doing what or how this case differs from another, revise them. Keep evidence distinctions, uncertainty and analytical meaning intact; readability is not permission to strengthen a claim.

Reader-facing database text must pass reader-copy-review before publication. Explain names, roles, decisions, mechanisms and consequences in ordinary sentences; do not assume readers know prior reports or internal shorthand. The required separate editorial pass repairs the stored fields and records final-text fingerprints, context and uncertainty checks in the run. Technical material belongs in explicitly identified methods/full-record sections.

Apply research-questions: explicitly weigh explanations, separate future likelihood/impact, and conclude how the evidence changes the assessment. Do not give negligible alternatives artificial balance or duplicate the causal narrative.

BELIEFS, REPUTATION AND FACTIONAL POWER
Assess factual truth and political effects separately. An allegation can remain disputed while its circulation, endorsement or rejection changes a faction's credibility, legitimacy, perceived competence, access or ability to mobilize support. Do not use "not proven", "no policy change" or "no representative poll" as an automatic reason to hold those political assessments. Public beliefs may dominate electoral and reputational consequences; formal authority, resources and implementation still require their own evidence. Conversely, an accurate finding may have little immediate effect if relevant audiences do not encounter or accept it.

For each consequential allegation, scandal, insider defection or major interview, establish: (1) the underlying claim and its verification status; (2) who actually said, amplified, accepted, rejected or acted on it, with dates and attribution; (3) the relevant audience and why its response matters; (4) the observed or reasonably inferred advantage/vulnerability now; and (5) the possible later consequences and an observation that would test them. Separate the existence of an attack, its reach, audience acceptance and subsequent behavior. A prominent interview is a documented intervention and may expose a vulnerability; views, repetition and the speaker's assertions do not establish audience agreement, representative opinion or defections. Use existing event context/rationale, assessment basis/watch and research-review explanations; do not create a second scoring system or duplicate the occurrence.

Use primary audio/video or a transcript when available; if only extracts or reporting were read, identify that scope and do not imply a full-episode review. Search reception as well as rebuttals and factual corroboration: relevant activists, voters, donors, organizers, commentators and officials; public defenses or distancing; attributed private concern; funding, staffing and cooperation changes. Record attempted versus unattempted reception coverage and what is self-selected or inaccessible. Polls can strengthen estimates of breadth but are not a prerequisite for a supported qualitative political judgment. Incomplete reception evidence limits confidence and claimed magnitude; it does not automatically erase a well-grounded vulnerability.

After claims of administrative corruption, obstruction or incompetence, also ask who loses credibility for selecting and supervising personnel. Examine the targeted officials, their faction/network, the leader who appointed or retained them, and rivals offering a different team. An accuser's attempt to shield the leader does not establish that audiences absolve the leader. Compare insider betrayal, implementation failure and leader-authorized policy explanations, and explain how each affects perceived management competence. Distinguish loss of office/access from loss of reputation or coalition trust, and from later policy or electoral losses. Personnel experience may offer transferable capability and may expose a candidate to scrutiny of prior hiring; do not automatically reward an outsider or a personnel specialist.

Conclude separately what is established about conduct, audience reception, present political vulnerability and future behavior. Weigh serious competing mechanisms and name what could reverse the assessment. Reflect supported conclusions in maintained faction and candidate summaries now, even when misconduct remains unresolved. Explain any numerical score hold by what that score measures; an unchanged authority score or daily nomination estimate must not imply unchanged credibility. Preserve the weekly odds cadence, authorized roster, original forecasts and evidence qualifications.
