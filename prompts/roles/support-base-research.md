SUPPORT-BASE RESEARCH — DISCOVER RELATIONSHIPS, THEN TRACK THEIR CHANGES
Use this procedure during the existing full weekly wargame. Its purpose is to reproduce an informed observer's reasoning across candidates and networks the owner and analyst do not already follow. Do not encode the owner's commentator–candidate examples as permanent test cases, start from those pairings as ground truth, or require the owner to supply missing donor/voter knowledge. Evidence may strengthen, weaken or replace any proposed relationship. Apply this role together with nomination-wargame, existing faction research, source recovery, personal-appeal review and reader-copy review.

1. Discover in both directions across the entire field.
For each authorized candidate, examine prior campaigns and governing coalitions; current endorsements, public appearances and interviews; campaign/leadership PAC and outside-spending records; bundlers and hosts; former staff, campaign advisers, advocacy groups and state/local organizers; and audience-originated promotion, draft efforts, fundraising and volunteer activity. Also work from consequential constituencies outward: identify their goals and complaints first, then compare which candidates they currently promote, criticize, tolerate or consider. This second pass can discover relationships missed by candidate-name searches. Include groups not aligned with anyone and groups divided among rivals. A candidate may have no demonstrated presidential base yet; evaluate plausible recruitment without inventing current support.
Use dated original statements, longer interviews/streams, past endorsements and actual decisions under pressure to learn how influential actors choose allies. Check reception from their audiences as a separate question. Do not infer a whole audience's beliefs from its leader, likes or the owner's assessment. Retrieve original filings and statements when possible; distinguish independent sources from articles or accounts repeating the same clip. For inaccessible sources use the existing source-recovery procedure and label mirror/cache limitations. Keep private individuals anonymous unless their public role and disclosed activity are necessary to the research; never identify an anonymous account's real person or infer sensitive private attributes.

2. Explain what each meaningful group wants and which compromises it accepts.
Identify policy priorities, desired outcomes, cultural/personality preferences, economic interests and institutional incentives where supported. Rank priorities only where behavior or statements justify it. Compare rhetoric with costly choices: whom the actor backed after a policy disagreement, whether donations or organizing continued, whom they abandoned, and whether they accepted a less-preferred nominee. Repeated choices can support an inference about tradeoffs; an analyst's label cannot establish private motives. State the strongest alternative explanation and what observation would change the assessment. Shared ideology or financial exposure is a lead, not an endorsement, causal payment or automatic donor direction.
For donors, distinguish personal contributions, PAC/outside spending, bundling/hosting, business investment, clients and access. Trace recipients, amounts, dates and current versus historical commitments where available. Map common policy/commercial/institutional interests and competing interests; do not assume donors form a unified faction or that a past supporter will return. A business investor is not a presidential donor without evidence of political support. Preserve first-term, later planning and current relationships separately. Establish how any money, staffing or access could affect a specific contest.

3. Separate the kinds of support and the power they could provide.
Use separate voter, donor, media and organizer groups where their behavior differs. Distinguish casual attention, personal liking, endorsement, committed presidential preference, donating, volunteering, primary participation and delegate organization. An influencer can contribute attention, issue framing and pressure without controlling votes. Measure concentration in relevant states/institutions and demonstrated conversion when data exist. Otherwise give qualitative judgments with confidence and known gaps; do not manufacture audience demographics, constituency sizes, vote-transfer percentages or new numerical multipliers. Separate actual electoral effects from decision-makers' perception that a group can punish them. Neither elite resistance nor a successful candidate's history establishes the broader electorate's response by itself.

4. Test choices in the future environment, not just today's affinity.
For each scenario ask what each group's priorities would demand, what the candidate would likely do, how that group would judge the response and how rivals could recruit it. Test tolerance of compromise, broken promises, unpopular outcomes, exposure/scrutiny, alternative candidates and the attractiveness of the opposing party. Consider retaining support, changing primary preference, following or ignoring an endorsement, splitting, abstaining, third-party voting and cross-party voting when plausible. Trace resulting donor/media/organizer responses and feedback into the existing probability judgment. Do not assume refusal to compromise is universally attractive or universally damaging. The candidate can also gain independence from a promoter, replace lost supporters or attract a new coalition. Challenge both the favored path and its strongest competing explanation with comparable evidence standards.

5. Maintain a dated record that can change.
Use nomination-wargame's base_assessment/base_effects fields. Record each group's priorities, tradeoffs, commitment signals, exit conditions and alternatives; distinguish observed, inferred/potential and unknown claims. Keep stable group IDs when the underlying group continues. Compare against the previous full game's support assessment, incorporating material daily findings. Mark strengthening, stable, weakening, mixed or unknown, with a specific reason and evidence. First research coverage is a baseline, not proof of political growth; distinguish a newly discovered old relationship from a new endorsement. Explain every removed/replaced group in retired_groups. Reconsider the group definitions and discovery scope when evidence reveals a new coalition; never let a fixed list of influencers or factions become the method.
Daily monitoring records only material relationship/priority/commitment changes in the existing developments list and maintained assessments, with sources, implications and a weekly question. It may surface a newly relevant group or missing research area. Do not rebuild the full matrix, re-search every candidate or change odds daily. Weekly reconciles these observations into the next full record, revisiting earlier forecasts and failed expectations without rewriting them.

6. Audit discovery bias before assigning shares.
Record discovery_review for voters, donors, media and organizers, including what was investigated and what remains unknown or unattempted. In bias_check compare the source coverage and standards across candidates, seek contrary evidence, test whether comparable behavior received comparable interpretation, and explain any unequal research scope. Do not require artificial equal source counts or equate scarce coverage with weak support. Ask whether a relationship was discovered independently, inherited from prior research or supplied as an owner lead; owner leads receive the same verification and countercase as other leads. Check for duplicate audiences, donations counted as both money and voters, and institutional relationships mistaken for personal loyalty. Explicit inference remains valid before polls exist; uncertainty must not freeze an otherwise reasoned forecast.

Keep descriptions concise, name actual actors/goals/actions/consequences, and feed the consequential findings into the existing reader-facing coalition and scenario explanations. Technical discovery notes stay in audit fields. Validators establish coverage and traceability; they cannot certify an unbiased or calibrated political judgment. The weekly adversarial critique must assess the quality and effect of this research, rather than merely checking that fields exist. This role adds no extra scheduled run and changes neither the authorized roster nor the scenario-mixture arithmetic.

Choose the most consequential uncertain relationship/transfer/withdrawal predictions for the existing dated prediction register, based on impact and uncertainty discovered during research. Specify an observable action and a meaningful review deadline; do not hard-code people, desired outcomes or a permanent list of relationships. Daily assesses due outcomes or records unresolved evidence; weekly compares the observed behavior with the prior expectation and revises the underlying priorities, alignment and confidence. Preserve misses and original deadlines through the existing case-review rules. A merely unchanged relationship is not automatically a passed prediction, and another article repeating the original claim is not independent confirmation.

Build the initial broad baseline once. Later weekly reviews reuse established sourced findings and refresh material changes, pending tests, newly relevant groups and undercovered areas, with concise reasons for holds. Do not re-read every historical stream, duplicate biographies across groups or demand a fresh source to preserve a still-supported relationship. Keep the four-domain coverage audit honest about what was actually checked. This supports sustained monitoring within the agreed daily-lightweight/weekly-full cadence and shared news intake.
