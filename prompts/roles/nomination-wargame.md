COMMON CANDIDATE STANDARDS — REQUIRED BEFORE WEEKLY WEIGHTING
Use the same questions and evidence thresholds for EVERY authorized candidate before choosing shares. A detailed biography or an arithmetic mixture does not establish that the evidence affected the forecast. Make the causal connection explicit. Do not substitute the user's enthusiasm for new evidence, and do not automatically raise McEntee or any other candidate as a correction.
1. Separate likelihood of entry from competitiveness IF running. Do not treat missing organization, platform and polls as independent penalties when all mainly reflect undecided entry or incomplete coverage. Distinguish unknown, searched-but-unverified, and documented absence.
2. Assess resources already controlled AND resources plausibly mobilizable: personnel relationships, donors, organizers, media distribution, political skills and geographic footholds. Compare transition staffing with electoral organizing honestly; neither equate them nor disregard transferable capacity. Apply the same future-building allowance to newcomers and established figures. Historical electoral failure, current office and name recognition all require interpretation rather than automatic bonuses or penalties.
3. Explain how combinations of assets could produce a breakthrough, and what breaks that chain. An audience, money, institutional relationships and a timely policy argument may reinforce one another. Conversely, access can fail to yield backing, an audience can fail to vote, and a candidate may never enter. Link each plausible strong/failure path to the existing candidate_debates rather than listing isolated credentials and then ignoring them in the numbers.
4. Compare coalition overlap, likely transfer, coordination and party route. Do not give competing candidates the entire same pool. A media constituency or preferred candidate differs from actual primary/delegate support. Map liabilities to affected voters, donors and organizers; a controversy is not a uniform numerical penalty across all constituencies.
5. Inspect every candidate's actual minimum/maximum scenario share. Explicitly defend a persistently low ceiling or high floor. A sympathetic narrative cannot substitute for a scenario in which a plausible breakthrough actually matters. Do not require equal ceilings or manufacture a favorable scenario for each candidate; explain evidence and conditions that justify different treatment.
6. Challenge comparable assumptions across candidates: for example, why is future organization plausible for one but effectively unavailable to another? Acknowledge where the evidence is stronger, where the gap is coverage, and what would reverse the relative judgment. Adjudicate before updating odds.
