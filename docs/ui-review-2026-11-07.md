UI review of Administration Ledger, November 7, 2026

The product should provide a straightforward single pane of glass showing the current Trump-administration power dynamics and the 2028 Republican nomination assessment. Current events, money, actions and positions support those assessments. Daily briefings supply updates and remain accessible as evidence and history.

The current interface makes the reader assemble that assessment. It foregrounds the research archive, operational records and generic data fields. The framework migration improved source organization, but its presentation hierarchy does not yet serve the product's purpose. This requires a redesign of the information hierarchy and assessment presentation as well as visual styling.

This review inspected the deployed revision `4425ccf` in Chrome at 1440×1000 and 390×844. It covered all seven main views, an issue detail, a faction profile, candidate detail tabs, person and donor dialogs, and event filters. Measurements describe those viewports and current content; they are not findings from a user study. Political claims were not independently re-researched for this UI audit.

The proposed first screen should have a compact application header, a literal title such as “Current assessment”, and a research-as-of date. Below that, two adjacent sections should carry the product: administration power on the left and the nomination race on the right. Their visual weight should reflect their importance, and both must be visible together on desktop.

The administration section should state the current balance of power in a short synthesis, followed by a small number of consequential decision contests. A row should answer: what decision is at stake; who has formal authority; who has access or leverage; which direction the balance appears to be moving; and why. “Authority”, “access”, “positioning” and an achieved “outcome” must remain distinct. An appointment announced for a future date must not be displayed as exercised authority. Unknown or contested influence should be stated directly.

The race section should show the five authorized candidates in a compact ranked comparison. Each row should combine the existing estimate with a short assessment of the candidate's route to the nomination, actual backing or organizational advantage, and chief vulnerability. A concise, always-visible note should state that estimates are subjective shares conditional on this five-candidate field. Full methodology belongs behind a clear link. Changes need a comparison period and reason; an unchanged estimate can simply say “Held” where that is clearer than repetitive `0.00 pp` entries. No weights, roster or methodology should change as a side effect of redesign.

A short section beneath the two main assessments should explain where the administration and nomination race interact. These should be reasoned implications linked to the underlying episode—for example, whether an actor's policy role creates both succession credibility and responsibility for costs. The UI should distinguish observed support from a hypothesis about future coalition transfer. It should not turn a forecast into independent evidence for a faction conclusion.

A compact “What could change this assessment” section should identify the few unresolved decisions or evidence gaps that matter most. This is about the conditions supporting the current judgment. The daily chronology should be available through a secondary “Evidence and recent developments” link rather than replace that judgment.

Navigation should follow questions rather than document types:

| Destination | Main question | Secondary content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current assessment | What is happening across the administration and nomination race? | Linked support and conditions that could change the assessment |
| Administration | Who controls what, who can influence it, and where are the contests? | Issues, faction profiles, key actors, money and relationships |
| 2028 nomination | How do the contenders compare, and why? | Candidate positions, coalition paths, backing, vulnerabilities and substantive history |
| Research | What evidence and review history support the assessments? | Daily briefings, weekly reviews, event ledger, sources, full people/donor directories, methods and operational provenance |

Keep global search, but make results identify the context: “JD Vance — person” and “JD Vance — nomination assessment” should lead to visibly connected profiles. A person may appear in several contexts; users should not have to discover those connections themselves. Preserve deep links, back navigation and filters. Opening evidence from an assessment should retain the assessment, the selected issue and the user's scroll position.

For Administration, default to an issue-specific power matrix or list of consequential contests. Factions should have summaries such as current objective, decision channel, leverage, constraint and key backers. Avoid an invented overall power score. Legacy numerical scores can remain in historical analysis with their existing caveats, but should not drive a new current ranking. Changing the look of a legacy metric does not validate it.

The graph should be optional exploration. Filter it to a selected issue or actor so that its links answer a concrete question. Relationship styling should distinguish a shared position, documented coordination, formal authority, a financial tie and an open dispute. Edges need dates and a short explanation on selection. Use explicit IDs rather than inferring entities from prose labels. Node size and position should have an explained meaning, or remain deliberately neutral. On mobile, a readable relationship list is preferable as the initial presentation.

For the nomination view, make the substantive candidate comparison the main content. Place the numerical distribution beside or above a compact explanation. Current weights have two decimal places because of normalization; the display should avoid suggesting a measured confidence interval. If rounded headline numbers are chosen, preserve the underlying values and show the exact figure in details. Displaying rounded figures is a presentation choice, not recalibration. Keep the conditional-field limitation visible.

The present history chart contains imported, consistency-review and workflow snapshots on the same date. A line connecting those snapshots offers little insight into political movement. Hide or replace the chart until there is meaningful substantive history; a small table of reviewed changes is clearer now. When the chart becomes useful, use research dates, explain what changed, and keep technical snapshots in provenance.

For full profiles, show a short explanation of the actor's role and significance before supporting lists. Use named, dated relationship rows for money: payer, relationship type, amount/period when known, recipient and the relevant decision channel. A historical contribution, business investment, lobbying client, public preference and confirmed campaign commitment must have different labels. Long profiles should have a few clear sections—assessment, actions and positions, money and alliances, evidence—not nested generic accordions for every object.

For evidence, use progressive disclosure with one clear level: claim or assessment, supporting episodes, then source material. Replace event IDs with meaningful titles and dates; resolve faction/donor/person references into links. Keep developer-style metadata in a full-record view or download. Repeated biographies, raw arrays and full snapshot objects should not appear in the normal reading path. Use the existing standard component library for the interactions; the problem is the choice and arrangement of content, not the availability of components.

The visual direction should feel like a concise analytical workspace. Use a warm neutral or quiet light canvas, dark high-contrast text and restrained accent colors. Make substantive conclusions the strongest type, supporting reasons medium emphasis, and source/date information quiet but readable. Use color for a small semantic vocabulary—strengthening, weakening, contested, unchanged—with text or icons so color is never the only signal. Separate evidence quality, completeness and analytical confidence rather than expressing all three through one badge.

Reduce the large marketing-style hero and repeated instructional copy. Remove decorative archive totals from the primary screen. Give percentages consistent right alignment and use a single compact comparative visual where it helps. Use a limited spacing scale and fewer nested bordered boxes. Keep longer prose to a comfortable reading width, roughly 60–75 characters, while comparison tables can use the full workspace. Aim for a clear editorial hierarchy rather than a wall of equally important cards.

On mobile, use a compact header with a search button or field that expands on demand. Replace horizontally hidden primary tabs with a compact menu or bottom navigation for the four main destinations. Lead with the administration synthesis and the race summary; all deeper evidence can follow contextual links. Use stacked, consistently labeled rows where wide tables would require repeated horizontal scrolling. Make filters a disclosure or sheet rather than a tall form above results. A full profile can use a page, while quick evidence can use a standard full-height sheet with a visible return control.

A maintained assessment layer is necessary to make this work. Today, the presentation pulls from event dates, older issue summaries, candidate rationale strings and arbitrary snapshot ordering. Styling cannot reconcile their freshness. Introduce explicit current-assessment records under `database/assessments/`, written and checked by the daily/weekly process. They should include the conclusion, scope, issue/actor references, formal authority versus other leverage, evidence references, research-as-of date, last substantive change and unresolved conditions. The UI should display “not reviewed” or “contested” when warranted; it must not manufacture an assessment from event counts, donor totals, graph centrality or legacy scores.

Display separate research and publication dates when needed. The current “Updated” label derives from event ingestion dates and cannot establish that every visible summary was reviewed. Select the latest substantive assessment explicitly. Imports, schema migrations and validation runs belong in operational history. Verify current summaries against newer evidence before promoting them onto the redesigned first screen; some source summaries still contain an earlier “hold to Nov 3” framing in a view presented as current on Nov 7.

Recommended implementation order:

1. Define and maintain the current assessment records, freshness rules and supporting evidence links. Resolve stale summaries and distinguish political reviews from technical snapshots.
2. Rebuild the first screen around the two core assessments; simplify primary navigation at the same time. Move counts, briefings and provenance into Research.
3. Replace generic profile/assessment rendering, add contextual evidence inspection, and connect person, faction and candidate contexts.
4. Apply the visual hierarchy and responsive layout across the main views. Make the faction graph a focused secondary tool and remove uninformative charts.
5. Verify the result with realistic reading and comparison tasks on desktop and mobile.

Acceptance targets for that verification: a reader can quickly state the current administration power assessment, name a consequential unresolved contest and identify the leading nomination candidate and chief vulnerability; explain the basis of a highlighted assessment with no more than two actions; reach a daily briefing without encountering it as the default product; distinguish current authority from access or positioning; distinguish the latest research date from publication; and navigate all primary destinations on mobile without hunting through a horizontal tab strip. The primary assessment views should not display internal IDs, migration notes or raw object dumps. Target comprehension within roughly ten seconds for the top-level assessment, to be tested with readers rather than asserted from layout alone.

This is a review and proposed direction. Product code, forecast values and deployment were not changed as part of this audit.
